# EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

## Article 5 - Prohibited AI practices
Certain practices are banned outright, including manipulative or exploitative techniques causing significant harm, social scoring leading to detrimental treatment, untargeted scraping of facial images, and emotion recognition in workplaces and education, subject to narrow exceptions. Prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025.

## Article 6 and Annex III - High-risk classification
AI systems used in listed areas are high-risk, including evaluating the creditworthiness or credit score of natural persons, risk assessment and pricing for life and health insurance, recruitment and worker management, education access, and essential public services. A high-risk AI system must meet the requirements of Articles 8 to 15 and be subject to conformity assessment. Application dates for stand-alone Annex III systems were provisionally agreed to move to 2 December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus on AI; verify the final adopted text before relying on a date.

## Article 9 - Risk management system
Providers of high-risk systems must run a continuous, documented risk management process across the lifecycle: identify and analyse foreseeable risks, estimate and evaluate them, adopt mitigation measures and test the system against defined metrics.

## Article 10 - Data and data governance
Training, validation and testing datasets must be relevant, sufficiently representative and as free of errors as possible, with documented design choices, provenance and examination for possible biases that could affect health, safety or fundamental rights.

## Article 11 - Technical documentation
Technical documentation demonstrating compliance must be drawn up before the system is placed on the market and kept up to date. It covers system description, development process, monitoring, performance metrics and the risk management system.

## Article 12 - Record-keeping
High-risk systems must technically allow automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system so that operation can be traced, risks and substantial modifications identified, and post-market monitoring supported.

## Article 14 - Human oversight
High-risk systems must be designed so natural persons can effectively oversee them during use. Overseers must be able to understand capabilities and limits, detect automation bias, correctly interpret output, decide not to use or to override the output, and interrupt the system with a stop control.

## Article 15 - Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity
High-risk systems must achieve appropriate and declared levels of accuracy, be resilient to errors and inconsistencies, and be protected against attacks such as data poisoning, model evasion and adversarial examples.

## Article 50 - Transparency obligations
Providers must ensure that people are told when they are interacting with an AI system such as a chatbot, unless obvious from context. Synthetic audio, image, video or text content must be marked in a machine-readable format as artificially generated, and deployers of deepfakes must disclose them.

## Article 99 - Penalties
Breaches of prohibited practices can lead to fines up to 35 million euro or 7 percent of worldwide annual turnover. Breaches of most other obligations, including high-risk requirements, can reach 15 million euro or 3 percent.
