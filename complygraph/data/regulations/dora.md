# DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) - Digital Operational Resilience Act

## Articles 5 to 16 - ICT risk management framework
Financial entities must maintain a documented ICT risk management framework approved and overseen by the management body. It includes asset identification, protection and prevention, anomaly detection, response and recovery, backup policies, and learning from incidents. Management bodies carry final responsibility.

## Articles 17 to 19 - ICT-related incident management and reporting
Entities must have a process to detect, classify, manage and log ICT-related incidents. Major ICT-related incidents must be reported to the competent authority through an initial notification, an intermediate report and a final report, within deadlines set in the regulatory technical standards. Significant cyber threats may be notified voluntarily. A platform outage or service disruption must first be classified against criteria such as clients affected, duration, geographic spread, data losses and criticality of services.

## Articles 24 to 27 - Digital operational resilience testing
Entities must run a risk-based testing programme at least yearly for critical ICT systems. Significant entities must perform advanced threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) at least every three years, using independent testers and covering live production systems that support critical functions.

## Articles 28 and 29 - ICT third-party risk management
Entities remain fully responsible for obligations even when functions are outsourced. They must keep a register of information on all contractual arrangements with ICT third-party providers, assess concentration risk before contracting, and have documented exit strategies and transition plans for providers supporting critical or important functions. Cloud and AI model API vendors fall within scope.

## Article 30 - Key contractual provisions
Contracts with ICT providers must describe services and locations of data processing, data protection and availability guarantees, service level descriptions, incident assistance, cooperation with authorities, termination rights and exit support, and audit and access rights. Contracts for critical or important functions require more extensive terms.

## Articles 31 to 44 - Oversight of critical ICT third-party providers
European supervisory authorities designate critical ICT third-party providers and oversee them through a lead overseer who may request information, run inspections and issue recommendations.

## Article 45 - Information-sharing arrangements
Financial entities may exchange cyber threat information and intelligence among themselves within trusted communities, subject to safeguards for business confidentiality and personal data protection.
