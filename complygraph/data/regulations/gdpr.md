# GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679)

## Article 5 - Processing principles
Personal data must be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently, collected for specified purposes, limited to what is necessary (data minimisation), kept accurate, stored no longer than needed (storage limitation), and protected with appropriate security. The controller must be able to demonstrate compliance with all of these principles (accountability).

## Article 22 - Automated individual decision-making
Individuals have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal or similarly significant effects, such as automated credit refusals. Exceptions exist (contract necessity, law, explicit consent) but require safeguards: the right to obtain human intervention, to express a point of view and to contest the decision.

## Article 25 - Data protection by design and by default
Controllers must build data protection measures such as pseudonymisation and minimisation into systems from the design stage, and by default only process the personal data necessary for each specific purpose.

## Article 30 - Records of processing activities
Controllers and processors must maintain written records of processing activities describing purposes, categories of data and recipients, transfers, retention periods and security measures, and make them available to the supervisory authority on request.

## Article 32 - Security of processing
Appropriate technical and organisational measures must be implemented, taking account of risk, including encryption, ongoing confidentiality and integrity of systems, ability to restore availability after an incident, and regular testing of the effectiveness of controls.

## Article 33 - Personal data breach notification
A controller must notify the competent supervisory authority of a personal data breach without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to result in risk to individuals. Processors must notify the controller without undue delay. Breaches must be documented internally.

## Article 35 - Data protection impact assessment
A DPIA is required before processing that is likely to result in high risk to individuals, in particular systematic and extensive profiling with significant effects, large-scale processing of special categories of data, or large-scale monitoring. It must describe the processing, assess necessity and proportionality, assess risks and list mitigating measures.

## Articles 44 to 49 - International transfers
Personal data may be transferred outside the EEA only on the basis of an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules, or a narrow derogation. Using a non-EU cloud or AI API provider for personal data therefore needs a transfer mechanism and a transfer impact assessment.

## Article 83 - Administrative fines
Infringements of core principles, data subject rights and transfer rules can lead to fines of up to 20 million euro or 4 percent of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Lower-tier infringements such as record-keeping and security failures can reach 10 million euro or 2 percent.
